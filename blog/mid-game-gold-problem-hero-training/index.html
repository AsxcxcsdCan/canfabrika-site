The mid-game gold problem and how Hero Training fixed it

Gold was piling up with nothing to buy. Here is how a training feature with an escalating daily price gave it a job without breaking the boss walls.

By Can · Can Fabrika · 21 October 2026 · 5 min read

Every economy needs a place for money to go. In Paw & Sword, gold comes in from hunting and boss fights and goes out on buildings, crafting and enhancing gear. For most of the game, that balance holds. In the middle of the game, it did not, and I only found out because a bot told me.

This post is about that problem, the feature that fixed it, and the rules that keep the fix from breaking something else.

How the problem showed up

I test balance with automated bot players that simulate a month of play for four kinds of player. One report line stood out: the idle free player's purse. It grew day after day through the middle stretch of the game, between the early rush and the later regions.

The reason was simple once I looked. In that stretch, the idle player had already built what they could, their heroes had decent gear, and the next big purchases were locked behind progress they had not made yet. Gold kept arriving from offline earnings and had nowhere to go.

A big number in the corner might sound harmless. It is not. If gold has no use, collecting it stops feeling good, and the daily check-in loses its reward. Worse, a hoard is a hidden balance risk: the moment something new unlocks, a player with a huge pile can buy through it instantly, which flattens the pacing you designed.

Why not just raise prices?

The obvious fixes all have problems:

  • Raise prices across the board. That punishes the active player, whose gold was already well used, to fix a problem only the idle player had.
  • Add more things to buy that do nothing. Decorations are nice, but a sink with no effect on the game does not restore the feeling that gold matters.
  • Let gold buy premium currency. That changes the whole economy and makes every other price harder to reason about.

What the mid game needed was a sink that scales with how much gold you have, gives something useful, and cannot be used to skip the parts of the game that are meant to take effort.

How Hero Training works

Hero Training unlocks at a Fame level a little way into the game. From then on, you can pay town gold to train a hero, and the hero gains a fixed share of the experience their current level needs. It uses the normal level-up path, so a trained hero gains Fame, stories and stats exactly as if they had earned the level in the field.

Three rules shape it:

  • The price rises with the hero's level. Training a high-level hero costs much more than training a new recruit, so the sink grows as your town does.
  • The price rises with every training of the day. The first session of the day for a hero is reasonable, the next one costs more, and so on, up to a small daily limit per hero. The counter resets each real day.
  • Training stops at the region cap. A hero can only be trained up to the top level of the newest region your town has opened.

Why those three rules

The escalating daily cost means a player with a big purse can still spend a lot, but with diminishing returns. You cannot pour a week of savings into one hero in one evening and double their level. It also gives the idle player a small, satisfying reason to check in each day: train a few heroes, see their bars move, leave.

The region cap is the important one. Training is for catching heroes up, not lifting them past where your town is. A new recruit who arrived late, or a hero who sat on the bench while others hunted, can be brought up to the group. But nobody can be trained beyond the current region's levels. That keeps the boss walls meaningful: they still check your gear and your team, not just your bank balance.

Heroes who are in a raid fight or in the rebirth ritual cannot train. That avoids odd edge cases, like a hero levelling up in the middle of a fight.

The companion change: an earlier Rebirth Sanctum

The same bot runs showed a second, related issue: heroes who had reached the top of what the current regions could teach them had nothing to work towards. The Rebirth Sanctum is where a hero can be reborn: they start lower again but keep part of what they earned and grow stronger over several rebirths. It used to open late. Moving it earlier gives strong heroes a next step, and gives gold and time another meaningful destination.

Did it work?

In the following bot runs, all four player profiles used training, and the idle player used it most, which is exactly who it was for. The purse now has somewhere to go in the mid game. Whether the price curve is right is something I will keep tuning; that is what the bots are for. But the shape of the solution feels right: a sink that rewards checking in, helps the heroes you neglected and leaves the walls standing.

Takeaway: when gold piles up, look for a sink that scales with the player's wealth, has diminishing returns within a day and is capped by progress. That way it gives money a job without letting money skip the game.
